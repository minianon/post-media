1/5 Your service calls a dependency that is down. Every request waits, times out and piles up. Soon your service is down too. A circuit breaker stops that. Explained in 5 tweets.

2/5 The idea: wrap the risky call in a breaker that watches for failures. Past a threshold, it trips and stops making the call at all. Same as the one in your home fuse box.

3/5 It has 3 states.

Closed: requests flow normally.
Open: calls fail fast, nothing is sent.
Half-open: a few trial requests go through to test recovery.

4/5 Why it helps: failing fast frees threads and connections, gives the sick service room to recover, and lets you return a fallback instead of a spinner. Pair it with sensible timeouts.

5/5 When the timeout ends, the breaker goes half-open. Trials succeed, it closes. Trials fail, it opens again.
