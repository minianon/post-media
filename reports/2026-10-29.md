How many projects should a fresher put on a resume?

I get this question a lot, and the honest answer is: fewer than you think.

Two or three projects you can explain well beat eight you can only name. A recruiter spends seconds on the page. An interviewer spends ten minutes on one project, and that is where you win or lose.

For each project, I would make sure four lines exist:

- The problem: who it helps, in one sentence
- What you built: the stack and the one core feature
- The hard part: one real bug or trade-off you had to solve
- The proof: a live link or a repo they can open

The hard part is the line most freshers skip. It is also the line interviewers ask about. "Why did you choose this database?" "What broke?" "What would you change?" If you built it yourself, you already have those answers. If you followed a tutorial step by step, you will feel it in the first follow-up question.

A few things I would cut:

- Clones with no change of your own
- Projects you cannot run today
- Long lists of technologies with no context

One project with a live link and a real story is worth more than a page of tutorial apps.

Which project on your resume could you explain for ten minutes without notes?

[1] (194 chars)
1/4 Two users hit "transfer" on the same account at the same moment. Both read balance = 100. Both write their own result. One update silently disappears.

This is a lost update. Here's the fix.

[2] (171 chars)
2/4 The fix is a lock. A database lock stops other sessions from changing a row while you work on it.

A shared lock is for reading: many transactions can hold it at once.

[3] (176 chars)
3/4 An exclusive lock is for writing: only one transaction can hold it. In SQL, SELECT ... FOR UPDATE takes that lock on the rows it reads. Other writers wait until you COMMIT.

[4] (202 chars)
4/4 The price: waiting. Keep transactions short, or the queue behind your lock grows.

More DBMS interview concepts in my 1Shot-DBMS notes: https://github.com/minianon/1Shot-DBMS
