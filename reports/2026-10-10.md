Most freshers lose the interview before it starts. In the projects section.

I've read a lot of fresher resumes while mentoring. The pattern that hurts most isn't weak DSA. It's lines like these:

"Built a full-stack app using React, Node and MongoDB."
"Developed an AI chatbot using an LLM API."
"Created an e-commerce website."

None of them give a recruiter a reason to call you, or anything to check.

What works better:

→ Lead with the problem, not the stack. "Ranks up to 10 resumes against a job description" says more than "Next.js + Groq app".
→ Add one engineering decision. What did you choose, and why?
→ Put a live link. A deployed project is easier to believe than a screenshot.
→ Write a README that explains the why. Some interviewers do open it.
→ Be ready to go one level deeper on every word. If you list Redis, expect "why Redis?"

As a fresher, the projects section is the one part of your resume that's fully in your control. Make it do the talking.

Recruiters and seniors: what makes a fresher's project stand out to you?

#2 · 12:00 IST · Thread (5 tweets)

1/5 Your service didn't go down because of a bug.

It went down because it kept waiting on a dependency that was already down.

That's the problem a circuit breaker solves. Quick breakdown 🧵

2/5 Remote calls fail, or worse, hang until a timeout.

If many callers keep waiting on one slow dependency, they run out of threads and connections, and the failure cascades upstream.

3/5 The 3 states:

CLOSED: requests flow. Failures cross a threshold → trip.
OPEN: calls fail fast, the dependency isn't touched. After a timeout →
HALF-OPEN: a few trial requests go through. Success → CLOSED. Still failing → OPEN.

4/5 Interview tip: when your design calls a third-party API (payments, an LLM, email), say where the breaker sits and what the fallback is.

Cached data, a queued retry, or a clear error. One line, and it shows you think about failure.
