Getting stuck in an interview is not the problem. Going silent is.

Interviewers cannot give you credit for thinking they cannot hear.

Here is the loop I would keep running when a problem feels hard:

1. Restate the problem in your own words. Ask about edge cases and input size.
2. Say the brute force out loud, even if it is slow. A correct slow answer beats a blank screen.
3. Find the cost. What work is being repeated? That is usually where the better idea hides.
4. Change one thing, not everything. Explain why you are changing it.
5. Test on a small example by hand. Bugs show up here before the interviewer finds them.
6. Still slow? Go back to step 3.

Why this works:
- The interviewer sees how you think, not just the final code
- Small hints are easier to give when they can follow your reasoning
- You stay calm because you always have a next step

It is also a habit. Practise it alone, out loud, on problems you already solved. It feels awkward for a week and natural after that.

Interviews reward clear thinking under pressure. The answer matters, but the path to it matters too.

Before you add a retry loop, check:

- Is the operation safe to repeat (idempotent)?
- Do you back off, with some jitter?
- Is there a max number of attempts?
- Does the error tell you how long to wait?

Retries without limits are just a slow outage.

#4 · 6:00 PM · Thread (5 tweets) · Thread (5 tweets) · System Design · long polling vs WebSockets vs SSE
🖼 https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minianon/post-media/main/2026/10-24/x-1800-1.png

(144 chars) 1/5 Your app needs to show live updates. The browser can't just wait for the server to call it. Three ways to push data, explained in 5 tweets 🧵

(213 chars) 2/5 Long polling: the client asks, the server holds the request open until there's news (or a timeout), then the client asks again.

Simple and works everywhere, but a new connection each time gets heavy at scale.

(209 chars) 3/5 WebSockets: one persistent connection after an HTTP upgrade handshake. Both sides can send any time.

Great for chat and games. Dropped connections aren't automatically recovered, so you handle reconnects.

(213 chars) 4/5 Server-Sent Events (SSE): the client opens a stream and the server keeps pushing events down it. One direction only, server to client.

Simple to use, and a good fit for feeds, notifications and progress bars.

(216 chars) 5/5 Rule of thumb: two-way and low latency? WebSockets. Server just pushing updates? SSE. Small project or old clients? Long polling.
