"Do I need to prepare for the HR round, or is it just a formality?"

Freshers ask me this a lot, usually the night before an interview.

It is not a formality. A shaky HR round can undo a strong technical one.

Here is how I would prepare, the same way you prepare for coding rounds:

- Write your intro down and keep it under a minute: name, education, what you have built, what you want next.
- Pick one real challenge and tell it in STAR format: situation, task, action, result.
- Have an honest weakness and say what you are doing about it. "I am a perfectionist" does not count.
- Know why you want this company. Mention something specific, not "great culture".
- Say your answers out loud, even record yourself. Reading in your head feels fine; speaking exposes the gaps.

One warning: do not memorise word-for-word scripts. Interviewers can tell, and one follow-up question breaks them. Practise the structure, not the sentences.

1/5 Your query is fast with 1,000 rows and painfully slow with 1 million.

The usual fix isn't a bigger server. It's a database index.  [134]

2/5 Without an index, the database does a full table scan: it checks every row to find your match.

Cost grows with table size. That's why it feels fine in dev and slow in production.  [183]

3/5 An index is a separate sorted structure (usually a B-tree) on a column.

The database walks the tree and jumps to the matching rows instead of reading the whole table.  [171]

4/5 The trade-off: every index makes writes a bit slower and uses extra storage.

Index the columns you filter, join and sort on. Don't index everything.  [153]

5/5 Run EXPLAIN on a slow query to see whether it scans or uses an index.

More DBMS notes for interviews: https://github.com/minianon/1Shot-DBMS
