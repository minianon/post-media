Should freshers grind DSA or build projects first?

This is probably the most common question I get from freshers. The honest answer: you need both, and the order matters less than the habit.

Why DSA:
- Many companies use it to screen in online assessments and early rounds
- It trains you to break a problem down under time pressure

Why projects:
- They show you can finish something real, not just solve puzzles
- They give you something concrete to talk about in the interview

Here is the loop I would follow:
- Daily: 1-2 DSA problems, then a short review of what you missed
- Weekly: ship one small feature of a project, live
- Share the link on your resume, README and in messages
- Review what stuck and what broke, then repeat

Small steps every day beat a three-week binge of one and nothing of the other.

If you only have time for one this week, pick the one you have been avoiding.

(124 chars) 1/5 Your query takes 3 seconds on 1M rows. Adding one index can make it near-instant. Here's how indexes work, in 5 tweets 🧵

(118 chars) 2/5 Without an index, the database scans every row to find a match. That's O(n), and it gets worse as the table grows.

(155 chars) 3/5 An index is a separate sorted structure (usually a B-tree) pointing to the rows. The database walks the tree, O(log n), then jumps straight to the row.

(181 chars) 4/5 The catch: every INSERT and UPDATE must also update each index. More indexes means slower writes and more storage. Index the columns you filter, join or sort on, not everything.

(205 chars) 5/5 Composite index (a, b) helps queries on a, or on a and b. Usually not on b alone. Column order matters.
