## LinkedIn

10:00 AM · daily (tusharbhardwaj2004ab) · Sat · Career lesson · cold messages · 1071 chars

🖼 https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minianon/post-media/main/2026/11-28/linkedin-1.png

Most cold messages get ignored for one reason: replying to them is work.

When I was an off-campus fresher, I kept noticing the same thing. A message that is easy to answer gets answered. A vague one sits in the inbox.

Here is the structure I would use today:

- Line 1: who you are, in one sentence. Name, college or role, what you are working towards.
- Line 2: why this person. One specific thing about their work, team or post. If you could send it to 50 people unchanged, it is too generic.
- Line 3: proof of work. One link, such as a project, a repo or a write-up. Not five.
- Line 4: one small ask. "Would you be open to a referral for this role?" is easier to answer than "Can you guide me?"

A few things I would avoid:
- Long life stories
- Attaching everything you have ever made
- Following up every day

You will still get silence sometimes. That is normal, and it says little about you. Keep the message short, polite and specific, then move on to the next one.

#2 · 12:00 PM · Thread (4 tweets) · Thread · System Design · replication

🖼 https://raw.githubusercontent.com/minianon/post-media/main/2026/11-28/x-1200-1.png

1/4 Your app has one database. Reads keep growing, and that one machine is now doing every read and every write.

One common fix is replication.

2/4 Primary-replica setup: all writes go to one primary. The primary copies its changes to one or more replicas.

The replicas only serve reads.

3/4 Why it helps: reads spread across several machines. If a replica dies, the others keep serving.

The catch: replicas can lag a little, so a read right after a write may return old data.

4/4 So for anything that must be fresh (like showing a user the profile they just edited), read from the primary.

My System-Design notes: https://github.com/minianon/System-Design

Which reads in your app can tolerate a little lag?
