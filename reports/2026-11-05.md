DSA or projects first? Freshers ask me this more than anything else.

My honest answer: do not pick one. Run both in a small loop.

Here is the loop I suggest:

- Daily: one DSA problem. Small and consistent beats a 6-hour weekend binge.
- Weekly: build one tiny thing and ship it. A tool you would actually use is enough.
- After each build: write a short README and add a live link.
- Then apply, and let the feedback decide what to improve next.

Why both?

DSA gets you through the online assessment and the first coding round. Projects give you something real to talk about when the interviewer says "tell me about something you built."

Only DSA, and your resume looks empty. Only projects, and you get stuck at the screening round.

The part people skip is the write-up. A project nobody can open or understand is worth very little on a resume.

If you are short on time, shift the split, but never drop one to zero. Small steps, every day.

Which side are you ignoring right now, DSA or building?

1/4 Your app is read-heavy and one database is struggling.

A common first fix: replication.

2/4 How it works: a primary accepts the writes and copies them to one or more replicas.

Reads go to the replicas, so the primary isn't doing all the work.

3/4 Bonus: if the primary goes down, a replica can be promoted so the system keeps running.

Backups taken from a replica also barely touch the primary.

4/4 The catch: replicas can lag, so a read right after a write may return stale data.

Where would you send reads that must be fresh?

Notes: https://github.com/minianon/System-Design
