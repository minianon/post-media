"Tell me about yourself" is the first question in most interviews, and freshers still fumble it.

The common mistakes:
- Reciting the whole resume
- Starting from school and going year by year
- Going silent after "I am a fast learner"

Here is a simple three-beat structure:

1. Who you are
Name, degree, and what you are focused on right now.

2. What you built
Pick one project or internship. Say what it did, not just the tech stack.

3. Why this role
Connect it to what this team works on. This is the part most freshers skip.

Then stop. About a minute is enough. A short answer gives the interviewer something to ask next, and that is where the real conversation starts.

Practise it out loud, not in your head. Written answers sound fine, but spoken ones reveal the gaps.

1/5 Add one server and half your cache keys suddenly point somewhere else. Consistent hashing fixes that. Explained in 5 tweets 🧵

2/5 The naive way: hash(key) % N picks the node.

But when N changes, nearly every key maps to a different node. Most requests need to be redistributed.

3/5 Consistent hashing puts the nodes AND the keys on the same hash ring.

A key goes to the closest node walking clockwise. Add or remove a node and only about K/N keys move (K keys, N nodes).

4/5 Catch: load can still be uneven, and one node becomes a hotspot.

Fix: virtual nodes. Each physical node gets several positions on the ring, which spreads the load and speeds up rebalancing.

5/5 Cassandra's data partitioning and DynamoDB's load distribution both use it.
