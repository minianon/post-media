Rejection is data, if you read it.

Most freshers I talk to treat a rejection as a verdict on themselves. It is closer to a bug report with a missing stack trace. Your job is to add the trace.

Here is the loop I would run after every no, or every application that gets no reply:

1. Find the round. Where did it slip: resume screen, online test, DSA round, project discussion, HR? If you do not know, write down your best guess.
2. Pick one gap. Not five. One thing you can name, like "I froze on a graph problem" or "I could not explain my project's database choice".
3. Practise it for a week. Out loud, timed, on problems you have already seen. Fixing one gap beats vaguely "preparing more".
4. Apply again. Not after you feel ready, after the week is done.

What I would avoid:
- Rewriting your whole resume after one rejection
- Starting a new course every time you get a no
- Going quiet for a month to prepare in secret

Off-campus hiring is a numbers game, but only if each round teaches you something. Ten applications with the same weakness give you the same result ten times.

Small steps, every day. One gap closed per week adds up faster than it feels.

Before you ask a senior for help, check:

- Can you state the problem in two sentences?
- What did you already try?
- What did you expect, and what happened instead?
- Can you share the error or a minimal example?

Good questions get fast answers.

(131 chars) 1/4 WHERE vs HAVING is a classic SQL interview question. Both filter, but at different moments. Here's the difference in 4 tweets 🧵

(116 chars) 2/4 WHERE filters individual rows before grouping happens. That's why it can't use aggregates like SUM() or COUNT().

(152 chars) 3/4 HAVING filters groups after GROUP BY, so it can use aggregates. Example: customers whose paid orders add up to more than 1000 (see the query above).

(193 chars) 4/4 Rule of thumb: filter rows with WHERE first (less data to group), then filter the groups with HAVING.
